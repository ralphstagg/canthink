Public Record Fact Sheet · Updated Oct. 26, 2026

Greg Abbott

Texas's Republican governor since January 2015, seeking a record fourth term against Democrat Gina Hinojosa on November 3, 2026. No arrest or criminal charge appears in public reporting, and no formal ethics finding against him was found. The main ethics questions concern large donors — including data-center-linked money — and withheld emails with Elon Musk.

Prior career

Abbott spent two decades as a lawyer and judge before becoming governor. The State Bar of Texas lists his law license (issued Nov. 8, 1985) as eligible to practice with no public disciplinary history, and the State Commission on Judicial Conduct's public-sanctions index covering his 1993–2001 judgeships has no entry naming him.

He was an attorney at Butler & Binion in Houston from 1984–1992. In 1984, at age 26, he was paralyzed when a tree fell on him while jogging. He was elected to Houston's 129th District Court in 1993, then appointed to the Texas Supreme Court by Gov. George W. Bush in 1995 and elected to it in 1996 and 1998. He resigned in 2001 to run for lieutenant governor before switching to the attorney general race.

As Attorney General from 2002–2015 — the longest-serving AG in Texas history — he personally argued and won Van Orden v. Perry (2005) at the U.S. Supreme Court, defending a Ten Commandments monument on the Capitol grounds, and became known for challenging Obama administration policies.

Record in office

As governor, Abbott signs or vetoes bills rather than casting votes. In 2025 he signed about 1,155 bills and fully vetoed 26, plus two line-item vetoes.

SB 2 (2025), education savings accounts

Gives participating families about $10,000 per child for private school, capped at $1 billion. Abbott calls it his biggest legislative win.

Property taxes

2025's SB 4 raised the homestead exemption from $100,000 to $140,000; SB 23 raised the over-65/disabled exemption to $60,000. He also says he pushed HJR 38 (2019), the constitutional ban on a state income tax.

THC

Vetoed the SB 3 total ban in June 2025, called a special session, and after it failed, issued Executive Order GA-56 (Sept. 10, 2025) setting age limits and enforcement standards.

Data centers

Recommended ending a major tax break in June 2026, paused grid approvals in August, and directed agencies to issue no new permits pending an ERCOT review.

In his own words

Abbott has not debated Hinojosa, though he debated opponents in all three of his earlier governor's races. His most recent on-the-record remarks found are an Oct. 22 rally and media Q&A in San Angelo and an Oct. 25 Fox News interview; earlier October stops fill in other topics. No full transcript was available for most, so this table relies on news coverage of those events. Border and immigration remarks from the final weeks were not found in sources reviewed, though his campaign has run border-security attacks on Hinojosa's votes.

Debates

In Tyler on Oct. 15, said campaign stops across the state are a better use of his time than a debate.

Property taxes

Proposes requiring two-thirds voter approval for any local property tax increase and cutting the appraisal cap from 10% to 3% a year; has called for abolishing school district taxes on homesteads.

Income tax

Told CBS Texas his goal with HJR 38 was to make a state income tax unconstitutional, and says Hinojosa opposed that.

Data centers

In San Angelo, said he halted new data center permits and set clear state standards; earlier said data centers should bring their own power and water.

Energy / economy

In Odessa (Oct. 13), tied property tax relief to oil and gas revenue and said people move to Texas to avoid income taxes.

Public safety

Said no Austin legislator is more hostile to law enforcement than Hinojosa.

Religion / Sharia

On Fox News (Oct. 25), said Hinojosa's CAIR endorsement shows she supports Sharia law, and pledged to ban "Sharia cities" and compounds. A separate news write-up noted that her vote against a related bill does not by itself show that.

Elections

Wants to close Texas's primary system.

Healthcare, THC, and vouchers

Abbott pitches a lower-cost insurance option rather than Medicaid expansion, favors regulating hemp THC over banning it, and touts the voucher program as a signature achievement.

IssueHis positionRecord and context
Healthcare Proposes an "Essential Benefits" health plan letting employers buy group coverage that omits some state-mandated benefits; his office projects premiums nearly 20% lower, about $1,150/year per family. Also proposes capping monthly copays for inhalers and epinephrine at $25. No bill language released as of July. Has long opposed Medicaid expansion; Texas has the nation's highest uninsured rate. Critics say thinner plans can leave workers paying for care they assumed was covered.

Arrests

No arrest or criminal charge involving Greg Abbott appears in the public reporting and biographies reviewed. His 1984 injury was an accident while jogging, not a legal matter. No debunked arrest rumor about him was found.

Ethics questions and controversies

The Texas Ethics Commission's published sworn-complaint orders for 2008–2026 include none against Abbott or his campaign committee. Orders before 2008 were not reviewed, and dismissed or minor-violation complaints stay confidential by law. No criminal proceeding was found. One civil case is pending: Hinojosa's Oct. 19, 2026 public-records lawsuit seeking unredacted emails with Elon Musk. The remaining issues are from news investigations into donors.

Data-center-linked donors

About $9M from data-center-related donors since July 2025, roughly 13% of his receipts; $1.5M came the month he proposed regulations and $2.8M after his August pause. $4.9M came from firms that build or run data centers.

His response
Campaign called the report a disingenuous attempt to manufacture controversy and listed his data center rules.
Musk emails

His office released about 1,400 pages to a regional news outlet, mostly blacked out, citing "intimate and embarrassing" content. Musk has given him $500,000.

His response
Office called Hinojosa's suit baseless and a political stunt; lawyers for Abbott and Musk had argued release would reveal trade secrets.
Donors as appointees

A 2022 Texas Tribune analysis found more than 70% of his university-board appointees had donated to him, and about $28M of his fundraising came from such appointees.

His response
In an earlier Tribune report on donor appointees, a spokesman called any suggestion of a link absurd.
Debate refusal

Declined all four TV debate invitations in 2026.

His response
Says campaigning statewide is a better use of time.
Hinojosa calls his donor ties a "corruption tax" and says he can be bought — a political dispute, not an ethics finding. His campaign says her accusations ignore his record on the border, public safety, and tax relief.
