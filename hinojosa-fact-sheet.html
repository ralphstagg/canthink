Gina Hinojosa

A five-term Democratic state representative from Austin and her party's nominee against Gov. Greg Abbott on November 3, 2026. No arrest or criminal charge appears in public reporting, and no formal ethics complaint against her was found. The main disputes are over how her tax and border votes are characterized in Abbott campaign ads.

Democrat Texas House since 2017 Born Dec. 8, 1973

Fundraising

Background
Born in the Rio Grande Valley (sources differ on McAllen vs. Mission); raised in Brownsville per her campaign

Prior career

Before the Legislature she practiced labor and civil rights law and served on the Austin school board. The State Bar of Texas lists her law license (issued Nov. 3, 1999) as eligible to practice with no public disciplinary history, and news coverage reviewed showed no complaint or misconduct finding from her school board tenure.

As a lawyer after 1999, she represented union members, including the Communications Workers of America, where she remains a dues-paying member; a 2021 profile says she specialized in workplace discrimination and wage theft. She also did legal-aid work and served as special assistant to Gov. Ann Richards.

She was elected to the Austin ISD board in November 2012 after her son's elementary school faced closure, and trustees chose her as board president in January 2015. The Abbott campaign says she voted for budgets that raised the average Austin homeowner's school tax bill by nearly $900 over four years; her campaign says she cut the tax rate as president. No independent check of the $900 figure was found.

Legislative record

Her signature enacted law is the 2017 public benefit corporation statute (HB 3488), letting for-profit companies adopt a stated public benefit alongside profit; it passed with bipartisan sponsors. Her campaign says she was House sponsor of a law securing nearly $1 billion in federal funds for Travis County indigent health care, though the bill number was not located. She authored the Fully Fund Our Future Act in the 88th Legislature (2023), and has been a lead House opponent of education savings accounts in both 2023 and 2025. She took part in the July 2021 walkout over elections bill SB 7 (her official bio says she organized it) and the August 2025 walkout over congressional redistricting.

In her own words

There has been no debate: she accepted four TV debate invitations and Abbott accepted none. Her latest major appearance was a live interview on CBS's Face the Nation from Austin on Oct. 25, 2026; other rows come from news interviews as noted.

Partisanship

Called her agenda populist rather than Democratic and said voters are disappointed in both parties.

Schools

Said Abbott withheld school funding in 2023 to push vouchers, and that the 2025 program moved $1B to private schools; ties school closures and four-day weeks largely to funding.

Health care

Wants every Texas child insured and a ban on bad-faith insurance denials. When pressed by the host, confirmed she supports Medicaid expansion but is not campaigning on the term.

Data centers

Would repeal the industry's tax breaks and give communities a say on siting; cites water, power-cost, and property-rights concerns.

Musk / corruption

Defended her lawsuit for Abbott–Musk emails; cited tax breaks for Musk companies, a $100M broadband contract, and a proposed Boca Chica beach sale. The host asked if targeting a major employer was risky.

Income tax

Told CBS Texas (Oct. 13) her 2016 comment favoring an income tax came before her time in the House, when she says she did not yet see the role of corruption.

Property taxes

Says she has never voted to raise property taxes and calls the Abbott ad false.

Abortion

Would sign a repeal of the ban and backs Roe-era access, but says it will not be her priority with a GOP Legislature; would not say whether she would sign a repeal of the ban on gender-affirming care for minors.

Affordability

Has proposed declaring a state cost-of-living emergency and sending $1,500 to households.

Border security and immigration were covered in an Oct. 23 Texas Standard interview, but no detailed account of her answers was available.

Healthcare, THC, and vouchers

Hinojosa wants every Texas child insured, would let voters decide on THC policy, and has pledged to end the voucher program — though she has hedged on whether a GOP Legislature would let her.

Reportedly told one outlet she is "not going to commit that I can do away with the vouchers" given the Legislature. 100,000+ students enrolled as of Oct. 1.

Arrests

No arrest or criminal charge involving Gina Hinojosa appears in public reporting reviewed. Searches surfaced an older DWI arrest of a different person with a similar name (a Hidalgo County district clerk), unrelated to this fact sheet. During the 2025 quorum break, state Republicans sought the absent Democrats' arrest to bring them back to the Capitol; no criminal charge against her was reported. No debunked arrest rumor about her was found.

Ethics questions and controversies

The Texas Ethics Commission's published sworn-complaint orders for 2008–2026 include none against her. Dismissed or minor-violation complaints stay confidential by law, so they would not appear. News searches found no criminal proceeding. The disputes below are a House disciplinary fine, a failed removal suit, and her own litigation.

Unpaid quorum-break fine

One of 17 House Democrats who had not paid fines (most about $6,000 plus $2,400 in DPS costs) imposed over the 2025 walkout; unpaid fines can be withheld from office budgets.

Her response
Her campaign did not respond to the reporting outlet's inquiry.
Removal suit

Attorney General Ken Paxton sued to declare her seat and about a dozen others vacant during the 2025 walkout. On May 15, 2026 the Texas Supreme Court refused to declare walkout Democrats' seats vacant in a related Abbott case.

Her response
Democrats argued the redistricting maps would disenfranchise voters.
Lawsuit for Musk emails

Sued Abbott Oct. 19, 2026 under a law letting legislators inspect confidential records. Abbott's office says she is abusing her official role for headlines and called the suit baseless.

Her response
Says she seeks the records for legislative purposes and has a right to see them.
Cease-and-desist letters

Her lawyers asked TV stations to pull a $12M Abbott ad, alleging defamation.

Her response
Says the ad is false; Abbott's campaign calls the letters an effort to hide her record.
Political disputes, not ethics findings: Abbott's campaign has branded her with mocking nicknames and imagery, and said her CAIR endorsement shows she supports Sharia law. She has answered with ads framing the race around cost of living.
