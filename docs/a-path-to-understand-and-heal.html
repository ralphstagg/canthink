A path to understand and heal

A few ways people manage overlapping conditions like MCTD, fibromyalgia, and hypermobility — sorted by how established the evidence is, so you can weigh what fits your life.

These three conditions are separate diagnoses, but they share symptoms — which is why days can feel confusing to sort out. Here's roughly how they overlap.

Where circles overlap, symptoms genuinely look alike — which is exactly why tracking your own patterns (and a specialist who knows the overlap) matters more than guessing.

Why it's confusing

Same symptom, different cause

Widespread pain and fatigue can come from an active MCTD flare (immune system attacking tissue), from fibromyalgia's amplified pain signaling, or from joint strain if hypermobility is part of the picture. The treatment for each is different — which is the whole reason a flare log and a specialist team pay off.

Pick the tier that matches today — not what you planned yesterday. Any tier is a complete session.

5 minutes · lying or seated

Flare day

  • Diaphragmatic breathing — 2–3 min, hand on belly, longer exhale than inhale
  • Seated ankle & wrist circles — 30 sec each direction
  • Loose band pulls — open and close arms slowly, no real resistance
  • Neck and shoulder rolls — a few slow rotations each way
This is a complete session on its own. There's no deficit to make up later.
10–15 minutes · seated/standing

Okay day

  • Seated band rows — anchor under feet, 8 gentle reps
  • Band chest opener — pull apart at chest height, 8 reps
  • Standing band leg press — 6–8 reps per side, skip if hips/knees flare today
  • Slow walk — 5–10 minutes
  • Box breathing — 2 min to close out (4 in · hold 4 · out 4 · hold 4)
15–20 minutes

Good day

  • Everything from "Okay day," plus:
  • Band bicep curls & tricep extensions — light resistance, 8–10 reps each
  • Light-weight arm raises — slow, 6 reps
  • Glute bridges — 8 reps
  • Extended walk — 15–20 min if it feels good
If you feel worse 12–24 hours after any activity, that's a sign to scale back next time — not to push harder.
