Public Record Fact Sheet · Updated Oct. 25, 2026

Ken Paxton

Texas attorney general since 2015, and the Republican nominee for U.S. Senate against Democrat James Talarico on November 3, 2026. Public records show one arrest — a 2015 securities fraud indictment resolved in 2024 without conviction. The central ethics question is the Nate Paul abuse-of-office allegations that led to his 2023 impeachment and acquittal.

Prior career

Paxton practiced law for about a decade before entering elected office; no bar discipline from that period appears in public reporting. He was an associate at Strasburger & Price from 1991 to 1995, then moved to in-house counsel for J.C. Penney. From 2002 on he ran his own McKinney firm — 14 years, per his official biography — focused on estate planning, probate, and real estate.

While a state legislator, he steered clients to a friend's investment advising business without registering with the state. That conduct led to a 2014 State Securities Board reprimand and $1,000 fine, and later became part of the criminal case against him.

Legislative record, 2003–2015

Paxton represented House District 70 from 2003–2013 and Senate District 8 from 2013–2015. His signature themes were state sovereignty and 10th Amendment arguments, lighter regulation, and abortion restrictions, which he authored or co-signed throughout his legislative career. He also voted for the law that made failing to register with the State Securities Board a crime — the same offense he was later charged with.

In his own words

Paxton has held no debate with Talarico. His most recent major public appearance was an Oct. 7 campaign rally with President Trump in San Antonio; this section draws on news coverage and campaign materials, not transcripts.

Debates

Told TMZ he would debate, but not with a CNN reporter moderating; an adviser called CNN's offer a set-up by "far-left activists." No voter town hall where he took direct questions turned up in recent coverage.

Immigration

Pledges to help finish the border wall and deport migrants with criminal records; emphasized an "America first" message at a July McAllen rally.

Abortion

Campaign site pledges to "defend the unborn"; has signaled openness to IVF restrictions but has offered no specific federal plan.

Health care

Proposes tax deductions instead of expanded government coverage — up to $25,000 for an individual's out-of-pocket costs, plus $25,000 per dependent. As AG he joined the multistate suit to overturn the ACA; in 2018 he said protecting pre-existing conditions was a job for state legislatures, not the federal government.

THC and hemp

No public comment found on Texas' hemp-THC ban in effect since July 31, though his office is defending it against industry lawsuits. Asked about it on camera, he said he didn't know the details and walked off. He has also sued cities, including Dallas, over marijuana decriminalization.

School vouchers

Supports Texas' $1 billion education savings account program, arguing it empowers parents without weakening public schools. He has sued school districts that discussed the program's impact, citing election-code limits on using public resources for electioneering.

Economy and taxes

Says the 2025 tax-cut extension will spur economic growth.

Election integrity

Has promoted a voter-fraud tip line and says he will work to stop illegal voting.

Arrests

Paxton's one arrest came from a 2015 securities fraud indictment, resolved in 2024 without a conviction or admission of guilt. No other arrest or criminal charge appears in public reporting.

July 2015 — Indictment

A Collin County grand jury charged him with two counts of securities fraud and one count of failing to register with state regulators, months into his first term. He was booked at the Collin County jail and released on bond. The allegation: he solicited investors for Servergy without disclosing the company paid him $100,000 in stock per referral. In 2014 he had called his failure to register a "paperwork error."

March 2017 — SEC case dismissed

The SEC's parallel civil suit over the same Servergy work was dismissed by a federal judge.

March 2024 — Resolution

A pretrial diversion deal required 100 hours of community service, legal ethics classes, and about $300,000 in restitution; charges were dismissed after 18 months.

Ethics questions and controversies

Formal proceedings on record: a 2023 impeachment (acquitted), a whistleblower lawsuit his office lost, a State Bar case (dropped), and a July 2026 complaint over his voter registration whose outcome has not been reported.

Nate Paul and abuse of office

Top aides told the FBI he used his office to help donor Nate Paul. The House impeached him 121–23; the Senate acquitted 16–14. The allegations centered on Paul allegedly hiring a woman with whom Paxton had an affair and paying for renovations to one of his homes. Some Republican senators said the evidence was thin and the House process rushed.

His response
Denied wrongdoing; called the impeachment a politically motivated plot.
Whistleblower firings

A judge awarded four fired aides $6.6M under the Texas Whistleblower Act; Paxton did not contest the facts and was never deposed.

His response
His office called it a "bogus judgement" from "rogue employees."
2020 election lawsuit

The State Bar accused him of making false fraud claims to the U.S. Supreme Court; the case was dropped in January 2025.

His response
Called the case meritless and a weaponized legal process.
Voter registration

ProPublica and the Texas Tribune reported he voted six times from a Collin County address after moving out in June 2024; Collin County Democrats filed a complaint.

His response
Campaign says he is a lawful, registered voter in full compliance with the law; he has declined further questions.

Whistleblower/impeachment timeline

2020

Eight aides report him to the FBI; all are fired or quit, and four sue under the Texas Whistleblower Act.

May 2023

House impeaches him 121–23 after he asks lawmakers to fund a $3.3M settlement with the whistleblowers.

Sept. 2023

Senate acquits on 16 of 20 articles (16–14 vote), ending a three-month suspension.

2024

Paxton stops contesting the whistleblower suit, so he is never deposed under oath.

Jan. 2025

Justice Department declines to pursue its federal investigation.

2025

Judge awards the four whistleblowers $6.6M, finding the office fired them for good-faith reports; Paxton later drops his appeal.

Some disputes cited by opponents — his 2020 election challenge, his debate refusals, and NRSC criticism of his conduct around his 2025 divorce — are political disputes rather than formal ethics findings. Supporters point to his acquittal and the dropped federal probe.

Attendance record

No critic's missed-vote claim against Paxton turned up in coverage, and his attendance has not been independently verified here. As attorney general since 2015 he casts no roll-call votes, so his only vote record is from his time in the Legislature, 2003–2015.

Open question: how his legislative miss rate compares with his chamber's average — verifying this would require pulling LegiScan's Texas session datasets and counting absences against House/Senate norms.
