James Talarico

Texas state representative from Austin and the Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate against Republican Ken Paxton on November 3, 2026. No arrests or criminal charges appear in the public record. The main ethics question is a 2022–2024 voter-registration address issue.

Teaching record

Talarico taught sixth-grade English language arts at Rhodes Middle School in San Antonio ISD from 2011 to 2013 through Teach For America, at a school in one of Texas' poorest zip codes that was underfunded and understaffed. No complaints, discipline, or misconduct from those years appear in public reporting. He went on to earn a master's in education policy at Harvard and led an education nonprofit before running for office in 2018.

A Texas Observer piece by a fellow Teach For America alum notes his campaign leans heavily on his two years in the classroom, and that his political committee's treasurer reportedly met him through that program.

Legislative record

His signature work is in public education; critics focus on his votes on guns, the border, and crime. He co-authored HB 3 (2019), the school-finance law that raised per-student funding, funded teacher raises, and paid for full-day pre-K for eligible 4-year-olds. He also sponsored HB 455 (2019) on standardized school recess, and filed HB 54 (2021), "Javier Ambler's Law," which passed.

Medical THC and cannabis

Talarico voted for every medical-cannabis expansion and marijuana-penalty reduction that reached a House vote from 2019 to 2021, and now supports legalizing regulated adult-use marijuana. His 2026 platform backs legal, taxed adult-use marijuana and expunging low-level marijuana records. In October 2026 he called the state's hemp-THC restrictions counterproductive, citing use for anxiety, chronic pain, and veterans' PTSD, and pledged to work for federal legalization.

School vouchers

A vocal opponent of vouchers, Talarico voted against Texas' education savings account program in both 2023 and 2025. In November 2023 he voted for an 84–63 amendment that stripped education savings accounts from HB 1 in the 4th special session, defeating Gov. Abbott's voucher push that round. In April 2025 he led House Democrats' amendment to put SB 2, the $1 billion education savings account bill, to a statewide vote in November 2025; Republicans tabled it. He has said Abbott pressured members by threatening their bills and primaries to defeat the referendum, which Abbott's office called "absolutely not true." He voted no as SB 2 passed the House 85–63 and became law in 2025.

Health care

Health care costs are a centerpiece of his record. In the Texas House he sponsored SB 827 (2021), capping insulin copays at $25 for a 30-day supply in state-regulated health plans — it passed 140–5 and became law. He also sponsored HB 18, a drug-savings program for uninsured Texans, which passed 144–1. Talarico has Type 1 diabetes and has said he paid $684 for his first month of insulin despite having insurance. In 2023 he authored HB 25, letting the state contract with Canadian wholesalers to import lower-cost prescription drugs, which passed with bipartisan support. He has also pushed for Texas to accept federal Medicaid expansion, which the state has not done.

In his own words — October 2026 town hall

At a 90-minute CNN/Jubilee town hall in Fort Worth on October 14, 2026, Talarico took questions from Democratic, Republican, and independent voters; Paxton did not respond to an invitation. Republican groups said his moderate positioning there contradicts parts of his House record.

Preexisting conditions

Supports federal protection for people with preexisting conditions.

Medical debt

Wants to ban medical debt from affecting credit scores.

Drug prices

With Mark Cuban, proposed antitrust legislation barring insurers from owning pharmacy benefit managers and requiring price transparency.

Abortion

Voted against Texas' abortion ban; supports federal protection for abortion rights. Said the Bible is silent on abortion and people can reach different conclusions; at the town hall he agreed with a Catholic voter who challenged him that both want fewer abortions.

Immigration

Opposes abolishing ICE but has called it a "secret police force"; says enforcement should target violent criminals, gangs, and cartels. Supports more Border Patrol agents, surveillance technology, more legal pathways, and a path to citizenship for long-term residents. Has said Biden failed to secure the border.

National debt

Calls it "intergenerational theft"; would close billionaire tax loopholes, end the war in Iran, and cut government waste.

Foreign policy

Wants the Iran war ended and no "forever war" in the Middle East; supports continued aid to Ukraine. Says Israel has a right to defend itself but blames Prime Minister Netanyahu, not Israelis, for civilian harm in Gaza; supports a two-state solution.

Transgender issues

Supports barring gender-transition surgery for minors but opposed Texas' broader bill on the subject. Has said his past "God is non-binary" remark was "provocative" and taken out of theological context.

Faith

Affirms the Bible as the ultimate source of truth; says Christians can disagree without questioning each other's faith.

Energy and economy

Backs an "all of the above" energy approach — protecting oil and gas while investing in renewables; calls himself a capitalist who opposes "crony capitalism."

Political reform

Backs congressional term limits, a ban on all super PACs, an end to gerrymandering, and a ban on congressional stock trading.

Project 2025

Outside the town hall, Talarico has opposed the Heritage Foundation's Project 2025 blueprint, tying it to Christian nationalism. In a September 2024 podcast appearance he called Texas a testing ground for its policies, citing the state's abortion restrictions, book bans, Ten Commandments classroom displays, and chaplains replacing school counselors. In a November 2024 post he warned the country is "sleepwalking toward theocracy." Project 2025 is a policy document, not legislation, so there is no vote on it.

Arrests

No arrest or criminal charge against Talarico appears in public reporting.

Ethics questions and controversies

No formal complaint or legal proceeding has been reported on any of the issues below.

Voter registration

He bought a home in June 2022 but kept his parents' address, about six miles away, on his registration until September 2024, voting from it in five elections. This was reported weeks after he accused Paxton of similar conduct. Legal experts said Texas residency law is broad and that a crime requires knowing intent.

His response
Says he followed all election laws; campaign declined timeline questions, citing threats.
Quorum-break fine

Fined $8,354 with most House Democrats for the August 2025 redistricting walkout; reported in 2026 as one of 17 who had not paid.

His response
Current status unconfirmed; no comment reported.
Remark about Colin Allred

Allred, his former primary rival, said — citing a Democrat's account of a January conversation — that Talarico privately called him a "mediocre Black man."

His response
Called it a mischaracterization; says he was criticizing Allred's campaign strategy.
Social media follows

Reports that his accounts followed and messaged OnlyFans models and escorts.

His response
Campaign says the social media team follows back large-following supporters without checking backgrounds.
His October 2026 absence

Social media posts claimed he had been treated at an Ohio mental health facility during a 12-day absence from the trail. The named facility, the Lindner Center of Hope, said he had never been treated there, and fact-checkers rated the claim false.

His response
Said he had the flu, at home in Austin; called the mental-hospital rumors conspiracy theories driven by a "rage economy."
Republicans also campaign on past statements, such as calling God "non-binary" on the House floor in 2021 and a 2020 tweet about racism he says was taken out of context. These are political disputes, not ethics findings.

Attendance record

Critics say he missed more than 842 votes from 2019 to 2025. Of the 5,808 House roll calls analyzed so far, he missed 94 (1.6%), against an average of 282 per member — ranking 99th of 130 for absences. He missed no votes in any 2023 or 2025 special session; 55 of his 94 misses came on two days in 2019. The 2021 quorum break produced no House roll calls, so that walkout did not add to his total.

Still open: the 2023 and 2025 regular sessions, about 5,000 additional roll calls not yet analyzed. Reaching the critics' 842 figure would require roughly 750 more misses there. The 2021 counts available do match the Club for Growth Foundation scorecard exactly.
